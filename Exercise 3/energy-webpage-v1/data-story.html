The Issue: The Hidden Cost of Screen Size

Consumers are buying larger televisions than ever before, but often lack awareness of how these massive screens drastically inflate household energy consumption and environmental impact.

1. Demonstrating the Issue: Size Preferences

The standard living room TV has grown. Our market data shows a massive concentration of models in the 140cm to 165cm (55" to 65") range.

  • aspect_ratio
    The Disappearance of Small Screens

    Smaller, more efficient screens are becoming rare as primary televisions, meaning baseline household energy needs are climbing.

2. Demonstrating the Impact: Energy Scaling

There is a stark, direct relationship between the diagonal size of a television and its annual running cost.

  • bolt
    Exponential Energy Growth

    Upgrading to a 75"+ screen pushes consumption upwards of 750 kWh/year. Screen choice directly drives the energy issue.

3. Exploring Nuance: Screen Technology

To overcome this issue without sacrificing quality, we look at display technology. Not all large screens draw equal power.

  • monitor
    LED vs. OLED

    While OLED panels offer great contrast, they frequently draw higher total energy averages than standard LCD (LED) equivalents.

4. Market Context: Industry Dominance

If we want to improve overall market efficiency, we need to examine who controls manufacturing volume.

  • pie_chart
    Top Brand Baseline

    Samsung, Kogan, and LG dominate available models. Any program addressing energy efficiency must target these key players.
