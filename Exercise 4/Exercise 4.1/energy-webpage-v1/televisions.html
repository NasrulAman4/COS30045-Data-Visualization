Smart Viewing, Sustainable Living

Televisions are a staple in Australian homes, but their energy footprint varies wildly. Discover how your choice in screen technology and viewing habits can impact both your electricity bill and the environment.

Screen Technology Efficiency

LED

star

The current standard. LED TVs use light-emitting diodes to backlight the screen. They are highly energy-efficient and affordable, making them a great baseline choice.

Energy Profile High Efficiency

QLED

star star

Quantum Dot LED. These offer brighter screens and better colors than standard LEDs. While efficient, their higher brightness capabilities can increase power consumption if maxed out.

Energy Profile Moderate Efficiency

OLED

star star star

Organic LED. Each pixel illuminates independently, allowing for perfect blacks. However, displaying bright, white scenes across the whole screen draws significantly more power than LED/QLED.

Energy Profile Variable / Lower Efficiency

The Energy Rating Label

In Australia, comparing TV efficiency is straightforward thanks to the Energy Rating Label. Look for two key metrics:

  • star
    Star Rating

    More stars mean greater efficiency compared to other models of the same screen size.

  • bolt
    Energy Consumption Number

    This figure (usually in kWh per year) gives you a concrete estimate of running costs based on average usage. Lower is better.

Actionable Steps to Reduce Usage

brightness_6

Adjust Brightness

Factory settings are often unnecessarily bright for home environments. Lowering backlight brightness can drastically cut power consumption.

eco

Enable Eco Mode

Most modern TVs feature an 'Eco' or 'Energy Saving' mode that automatically adjusts brightness based on ambient room light.

power_settings_new

Avoid Standby

While standby power is lower than it used to be, switching off the TV at the wall when not in use for extended periods ensures zero energy drain.

straighten

Right-Size Your Screen

A larger screen requires more power to illuminate. Consider buying a TV sized appropriately for your viewing distance rather than defaulting to the largest option.
